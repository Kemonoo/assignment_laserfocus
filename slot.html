Process & methodology · Part 02

Industrial Sketchbook Slot Prototype

What I built

A playable, self-contained 2.5D slot machine with a draggable, clickable, and tappable lever; sequential reel stops; credits and result feedback; sound; and coins that appear in the tray after a win. The complete outcome is calculated before the animation starts, so the symbols shown always match the game result.

Wins, rewards & probability

A win requires three identical symbols on the marked center payline. Payouts are Seven 500×, Diamond 100×, Bell 40×, Cherry 16×, and Lemon 4×. Every mode uses 20 virtual stops per reel—8,000 equally likely three-reel combinations—but changes the symbol distribution to create a different risk profile.

Friendly 14.425% hit · 96.0% RTP Classic 11.125% hit · 91.2% RTP High Roller 9.700% hit · 92.0% RTP Win condition 3 matching center symbols

The notebook's math panel updates when the mode changes. I enumerated all 8,000 outcomes for each setup to verify its hit rate and theoretical return rather than relying only on simulation.

How it works & source

  • Lightweight implementation: The cabinet, reels, lever, symbols, and coins are separate HTML/CSS layers animated with vanilla JavaScript, avoiding a heavier WebGL build.
  • Clear anticipation: The engine selects the three stops first, then lands the reels one by one with a longer final delay.
  • Shareable delivery: The prototype and its source are available in the linked repository and can run as a normal web page.

AI workflow & design decisions

ChatGPT / Codex helped translate the brief into game logic, build and revise the prototype, enumerate probabilities, adapt visual layers, and correct the reel, perspective, lever, mobile, and audio behavior. Claude Design supported interface exploration and the notebook layout, while Gemini supported planning and concept cross-checking.

The first generic dark-dashboard direction was rejected. I compared a clean monochrome interface with an industrial sketchbook concept, chose the sketchbook because it explains the design as an experiment, and selected layered 2.5D over full 3D to keep the result tactile, lightweight, and easy to share. I provided the art direction, references, mechanical feedback, and final judgment throughout each test-and-revision cycle.

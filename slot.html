Process & methodology · Part 02

Industrial Sketchbook Slot Prototype

Concept & art direction

Instead of presenting another flat casino interface, I treated the prototype as a living industrial-design document. Handwritten annotations, construction lines, mechanical callouts, and warm paper textures make the game logic part of the visual story.

Technical & mechanical architecture

  • Layered 2.5D construction: The cabinet, reels, lever, symbols, and coin tray are isolated visual layers animated with HTML, CSS transforms, and vanilla JavaScript rather than a heavy WebGL framework.
  • Mechanical lever trajectory: The lever follows the side of the cabinet like a crank mechanism. It supports drag-to-pull as well as click or tap interaction for desktop and mobile.
  • Pre-calculated stopping logic: The engine chooses the complete outcome before rotation begins. The reels then stop sequentially, creating anticipation while keeping the displayed result consistent with the mathematical result.

Mathematical model

Each reel has 20 virtual stops: one Seven, two Diamonds, three Bells, five Cherries, and nine Lemons. Three independently sampled reels therefore produce 20³, or 8,000, equally likely stop combinations. Only three matching symbols on the marked center line win.

Winning outcomes 890 / 8,000 Hit frequency 11.125% Theoretical RTP 91.2% House edge 8.8%

Payout multipliers are Seven 500×, Diamond 100×, Bell 40×, Cherry 16×, and Lemon 4×. These values are calculated from the same configuration used by the playable machine rather than being decorative labels.

AI toolchain & iteration

  • Gemini: strategic planning, concept evaluation, workflow architecture, and mathematical cross-checking.
  • Claude Design: interface exploration, layout assembly, and the final notebook presentation.
  • ChatGPT / Codex: game logic, probability verification, 2.5D asset separation, perspective and mechanical corrections, responsive interaction, and audio integration.

I directed the process by selecting the art direction, rejecting weak iterations, supplying mechanical feedback, and testing whether the visuals clearly matched the calculated outcomes.
